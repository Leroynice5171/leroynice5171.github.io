## 📋 What Does Kage Check?

Kage performs a comprehensive analysis of your computer. Here's what it examines:

### 🔍 Artefact Collection
Kage gathers important system information and files that can reveal what's been happening on your computer. This includes recent file activity, program usage, and system events.

### ⏰ Sigma-Correlated Timeline
Creates a clear timeline of events on your computer. Kage uses industry-standard Sigma rules to match suspicious patterns and show you exactly what happened and when.

### 🦠 YARA Verdicts
YARA is a pattern-matching tool used by security experts worldwide. Kage uses it to scan your files for known malicious patterns. Each file gets a clear verdict: safe or suspicious.

### 🌐 Open Sockets & Processes
Shows you which programs on your computer are communicating with the internet right now. If something looks unusual—like an unknown program sending data—Kage will flag it.

### 👤 Local Accounts
Lists all user accounts on your computer, helping you spot any accounts you didn't create. This is critical because hackers often create hidden accounts to access your system.

### 💾 Disk Root Anomalies
Checks for strange files or folders in system directories that shouldn't be there. This is a common place for malware to hide.

### 🚨 Logging Blind Spots
Not all computers log everything they should. Kage identifies gaps in your system's logging, so you know what information isn't being tracked. This is vital—you can't investigate what you didn't record.

### 🌍 Indicator Reputation
Checks suspicious files and IP addresses against known reputation databases. If something has been flagged before, Kage will tell you.

### 📊 Calibrated Risk Score
Finally, Kage combines all findings into a single, easy-to-understand risk score from 0 to 100. A low score means your computer is healthy. A high score means you need to take action.
